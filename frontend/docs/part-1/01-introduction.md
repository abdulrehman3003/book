# Introduction to Physical AI

## What is Physical AI?

Physical AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that interact with and operate in the physical world. Unlike purely digital AI systems that process data in virtual environments, Physical AI must:

- **Sense** the environment through physical sensors
- **Understand** spatial relationships and physical constraints
- **Act** through actuators and motors
- **Adapt** to real-world uncertainty and dynamics

## Embodied Intelligence vs. Digital AI

| Aspect | Digital AI | Physical AI |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Environment** | Virtual/Digital | Physical World |
| **Perception** | Structured data | Sensors (cameras, LIDAR, IMU) |
| **Action** | Data output | Physical actuation |
| **Constraints** | Computational | Physics, safety, real-time |
| **Feedback** | Immediate | Delayed, noisy |

## Humanoid vs. Non-Humanoid Robots

### Humanoid Robots
- Human-like form factor
- Designed for human environments
- Complex bipedal locomotion
- Natural human-robot interaction

### Non-Humanoid Robots
- Task-specific designs (quadrupeds, wheeled, aerial)
- Often more efficient for specific tasks
- Simpler control systems
- Examples: Boston Dynamics Spot, warehouse robots

## The Importance of Physical Embodiment

Physical embodiment is crucial because:

1. **Real-world interaction**: Many tasks require physical presence
2. **Sensorimotor learning**: Intelligence emerges from interaction
3. **Safety-critical applications**: Healthcare, disaster response
4. **Human collaboration**: Working alongside humans in shared spaces

## Applications

- **Manufacturing**: Assembly, inspection, logistics
- **Healthcare**: Surgery assistance, patient care
- **Exploration**: Space, underwater, hazardous environments
- **Service**: Hospitality, retail, home assistance
